I am an evolutionary biologist and invertebrate physiologist interested in how complex phenotypes evolve and diversify, and how animals respond to temporally dynamic environments. As such, my main focus is understanding the evolution and organismal consequences of biological rhythms. I study these topics using -omics, behavioral experiments, and evolutionary comparative methods in a variety of systems, but I mainly work with the the beautiful, diverse and ancient phlyum Cnidaria (jellyfish, corals, and their relatives). 

I am currently an NIH Postdoctoral Fellow at the University of California, Santa Barbara, where I study the interactions between circadian rhythms and regeneration in my favorite model system: the starlet sea anemone _Nematostella vectensis_. I completed my Ph.D. in Dr.[Ann Tarrant](https://www2.whoi.edu/site/tarrantlab/)’s lab at the Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution, with a dissertation on circadian rhythms and metabolic responses of marine invertebrates. I then came to UCSB as a postdoc to work with Dr. [Todd Oakley](https://labs.eemb.ucsb.edu/oakley/todd/), where I studied light perception systems and genomic evolution in jellyfish. Check out [our recent paper from this work in _PNAS_!](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2616248123)

Please [reach out](mail:cberger@ucsb.edu) if you are interested in collaborating. My interests are broad and I'm always happy to chat!
