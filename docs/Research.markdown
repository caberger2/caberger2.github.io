At a high level, I am interested in fundamental questions in evolutionary biology, including What are the constraints governing genome evolution, and how do these shape the relationships between genomes and phenomes? To fully address these questions, we also need to understand how things work at the organismal and molecular levels, which is why I take an integrative approach to research. 




I am an evolutionary biologist and invertebrate physiologist interested in how complex phenotypes evolve and diversify, and how animals respond to temporally dynamic environments. As such, my main interest is understanding the evolution and organismal consequences of biological rhythms. I study these topics using many systems and methods, but I mainly focus on the beautiful, diverse and ancient phlyum Cnidaria (jellyfish, corals, and their relatives). 
I believe communication across molecular, organismal, and evolutionary scales is key to a fuller understanding of biology. Evolutionary patterns are best understood when grounded in organismal biology, including physiological processes and ecological interactions. Conversely, mechanisms learned from studying model systems can be generalized and extended using phylogenetics and comparative methods. My research seeks to integrate these perspectives using comparative -omics approaches, phylogenetics, and bioinformatics with behavioral and physiological experiments.

This topic has broad relevance to human health (because circadian disruptions are linked to all sorts of metabolic, cardiovascular, and psychological problems) and to the effects of climate change on natural ecosystems (because light pollutition, global warming, and other human impacts are changing temporal patterns of environmental variation).

Cnidarians are an important and under-utilized study system in circadain biology because 1) their evolutionary position is key to understand how clocks first arose and subsequently diversified in animals; and 2) their simple body plans and lack of a central nervous system make it is easier to study the physiological principles of circadain rhythms.
